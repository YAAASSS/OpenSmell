# RFC-0001: OpenSmell Vision and Scope

- **Status:** Draft
- **Version:** 0.1
- **Date:** September 2026

## Abstract

OpenSmell is an open interoperability framework for digital olfaction.

Its purpose is to provide a hardware-independent and odor-model-independent
way to represent, exchange, and request the rendering of digital olfactory
information across applications and olfactory devices.

OpenSmell does not attempt to define the biological, perceptual, or chemical
nature of smell. It does not prescribe how an odor must be sensed, encoded,
predicted, or physically reproduced.

Instead, OpenSmell defines interfaces through which different odor
representations, software systems, scientific models, and olfactory rendering
devices can interoperate.

The core principle of OpenSmell is:

> **OpenSmell describes interfaces, not olfactory truth.**

---

## 1. Motivation

Digital olfaction is an emerging field involving multiple independent areas
of research and engineering, including:

- electronic noses and chemical sensing;
- molecular odor databases;
- machine-learning models for odor prediction;
- perceptual odor representations;
- olfactory displays and scent delivery devices;
- multisensory applications;
- digital transmission of olfactory information.

Several projects and standards already address parts of this ecosystem.

Examples include MPEG-V for sensory effect representation and device
capabilities, OWidgets for device-independent olfactory experience design,
OpenPOM for computational odor representations, OdorNet for molecular
olfactory data, and research projects such as D2Smell investigating the
digitization and remote reproduction of smell.

However, these systems address different layers of the problem.

OpenSmell explores the need for an open interoperability layer between:

1. digital odor representations;
2. applications consuming or producing those representations;
3. systems capable of translating them;
4. olfactory rendering devices.

OpenSmell is not intended to replace existing standards or scientific work.
Where possible, it should interoperate with them.

---

## 2. Problem Statement

There is currently no universally adopted representation equivalent to RGB
for digital olfaction.

An application may describe an odor semantically:

    coffee

A scientific model may represent an odor as a multidimensional vector:

    [0.18, 0.72, 0.04, ...]

A future olfactory reproduction system may represent the same odor as a
mixture of odor primaries:

    primary-001 = 0.32
    primary-007 = 0.71

A scent delivery device may instead expose a fixed set of cartridges:

    cartridge-1 = coffee
    cartridge-2 = vanilla
    cartridge-3 = smoke

These representations are not equivalent and should not be treated as if
they were.

OpenSmell aims to provide a common framework capable of transporting and
identifying different odor representations without assuming that any single
representation is scientifically definitive.

---

## 3. Goals

OpenSmell aims to:

### 3.1 Provide a common digital odor container

Define a portable and extensible format capable of carrying different kinds
of digital olfactory representations.

### 3.2 Remain odor-model independent

OpenSmell must not depend on one specific scientific model of olfaction.

New representations should be introducible without redesigning the entire
protocol.

### 3.3 Remain hardware independent

An OpenSmell representation should not be tied to one manufacturer or one
olfactory display technology.

### 3.4 Separate odor identity from rendering instructions

The representation of an odor and the instructions used to render it are
different concepts.

For example:

    odor = coffee

is conceptually separate from:

    intensity = 0.75

The first describes what is being represented.

The second describes how an application requests that representation to be
rendered.

### 3.5 Enable device adapters

Hardware-specific behavior should be implemented through adapters rather
than embedded into the core representation.

Conceptually:

    Application
        |
        v
    OpenSmell
        |
        v
    Renderer / Mapper
        |
        v
    Device Interface
        |
        v
    Device Adapter
        |
        v
    Olfactory Device

### 3.6 Support graceful approximation

A device may not be capable of reproducing an odor exactly.

OpenSmell should eventually allow a rendering system to report whether an
odor can be:

- rendered exactly;
- approximated;
- partially rendered;
- not rendered.

The method used to calculate an approximation is outside the scope of the
initial specification.

---

## 4. Non-Goals

OpenSmell does not aim to:

### 4.1 Define the biological nature of smell

OpenSmell is not a theory of human olfaction.

### 4.2 Define a universal odor space

The project does not claim that any particular vector space, descriptor
system, molecular representation, or set of odor primaries is the definitive
representation of smell.

### 4.3 Design scent-generation hardware

OpenSmell may communicate with olfactory devices but does not specify their
mechanical or chemical construction.

### 4.4 Invent odor synthesis algorithms

Algorithms converting one odor representation into another may exist as
external models, libraries, or plugins.

They are not part of OpenSmell Core.

### 4.5 Replace existing standards

OpenSmell should investigate compatibility with existing work such as
MPEG-V rather than unnecessarily duplicating it.

### 4.6 Provide timeline or multimedia synchronization in the Core

Scheduling scent events for video, games, VR, or other timed experiences is
useful but is not required for the core interoperability problem.

Such functionality may later be implemented as optional extensions.

## 6. Odor Representation Classes

OpenSmell initially considers three broad representation classes.

These classes are conceptual and are not yet a finalized schema.

### 6.1 Semantic Representation

Human-readable odor information.

Example:

    type: semantic
    name: coffee
    descriptors:
      - roasted
      - woody

Semantic representations may be useful for classification, search,
applications, and devices containing predefined scent cartridges.

They do not necessarily contain enough information to physically reproduce
an odor.

### 6.2 Perceptual Representation

A representation produced by a perceptual or computational model.

Example:

    type: perceptual
    model: example-model
    vector:
      - 0.18
      - 0.72
      - 0.04

OpenSmell does not define the meaning of such vectors.

The model identifier determines how the representation should be
interpreted.

### 6.3 Renderable Representation

A representation containing information intended for physical reproduction.

For example:

    type: mixture
    model: example-primary-system
    components:
      primary-001: 0.32
      primary-007: 0.71

The exact semantics of such representations will depend on the reproduction
model being used.

OpenSmell must therefore identify the representation model rather than
assuming a universal set of odor primaries.

---

## 7. Rendering

Rendering is the process of attempting to translate an OpenSmell odor
representation into something a target system or device can execute.

A rendering request may contain parameters such as:

- intensity;
- duration;
- device selection;
- rendering preferences.

These parameters are not intrinsic properties of the odor representation.

For example:

    render(
        odor=coffee,
        intensity=0.75
    )

The same odor representation could therefore be rendered differently by
different applications or devices.

---

## 8. Mapping

A mapper translates an odor representation or rendering request into a form
supported by a target device.

For example, a future device might support a specific set of odor primaries.

If an OpenSmell representation uses the same primary system, mapping may be
direct.

If the device supports only predefined cartridges, the mapper may attempt to
select the closest available scent.

The mapping mechanism is deliberately separated from the odor
representation itself.

---

## 9. Device Interface

OpenSmell should expose a common interface for olfactory rendering devices.

A conceptual API could eventually resemble:

    device.capabilities()

    device.can_render(odor)

    device.render(odor)

    device.stop()

Hardware-specific protocols should remain inside device adapters.

This allows applications to interact with OpenSmell rather than directly
depending on manufacturer-specific SDKs.

---

## 10. Scheduling

Scheduling is intentionally excluded from OpenSmell Core.

Systems such as games, films, VR applications, simulations, or interactive
installations may need to trigger odors according to a timeline.

This functionality can be implemented above the OpenSmell Core layer.

For example:

    OpenSmell Timeline
            |
            v
      OpenSmell Core
            |
            v
          Device

This keeps the core interoperability layer small and avoids coupling
OpenSmell to a particular multimedia use case.

---

## 11. Relationship to Existing Work

OpenSmell must acknowledge and learn from existing digital olfaction
research and standards.

### MPEG-V

MPEG-V defines mechanisms for representing sensory effects and device
capabilities, including olfactory effects.

OpenSmell does not intend to replace MPEG-V.

Future work should investigate import/export or adapter mechanisms between
OpenSmell and MPEG-V representations.

### OWidgets

OWidgets demonstrated device-independent olfactory experience design and
introduced useful architectural concepts including mapping and uniform
device interfaces.

OpenSmell adopts similar interoperability principles while focusing on the
transport and interpretation of digital odor representations.

Timeline scheduling is intentionally kept outside OpenSmell Core.

### OpenPOM

OpenPOM provides an open implementation related to computational odor
mapping and prediction.

OpenSmell may eventually carry representations produced by models such as
OpenPOM but does not depend on OpenPOM.

### OdorNet

OdorNet provides structured molecular and olfactory label data.

Such datasets may be useful for testing, examples, model integration, and
future representation conversion.

OpenSmell does not duplicate these datasets.

### D2Smell

D2Smell investigates fundamental methods for digitizing and reproducing
olfactory perception.

OpenSmell should remain compatible with the possibility that projects such
as D2Smell introduce new digital odor representations in the future.

---

## 12. OpenSmell Core

The initial OpenSmell Core should remain deliberately small.

Version 0.1 should demonstrate only the following pipeline:

    .osmell file
         |
         v
       Parser
         |
         v
     Validation
         |
         v
    Odor Object
         |
         v
      Renderer
         |
         v
       Mapper
         |
         v
    Device Adapter
         |
         v
     Simulator

No physical scent hardware is required for the first implementation.

The simulator will allow the architecture and API to be tested before
support for real devices is introduced.

## 13. Design Principles

OpenSmell development should follow these principles.

### Principle 1 — Interfaces, not olfactory truth

OpenSmell describes how systems exchange olfactory information.

It does not define the scientifically correct representation of smell.

### Principle 2 — Extensibility

New odor representations and device types should be addable without breaking
existing implementations.

### Principle 3 — Hardware independence

Core representations must not depend on one manufacturer's hardware.

### Principle 4 — Model independence

Core representations must not depend on one scientific odor model.

### Principle 5 — Separation of concerns

Odor representation, rendering, mapping, device communication, and
application-level scheduling are separate responsibilities.

### Principle 6 — Minimal Core

Features should only enter OpenSmell Core when they are necessary for
interoperability.

Everything else should preferably be implemented as an extension.

### Principle 7 — Compatibility before reinvention

Existing standards, research projects, and device APIs should be integrated
where practical rather than unnecessarily replaced.

---

## 14. Initial Milestone

The first implementation milestone is **OpenSmell 0.1 — Proof of
Architecture**.

A minimal implementation should eventually allow:

    import opensmell

    odor = opensmell.load("coffee.osmell")

    device = opensmell.Simulator()

    device.render(odor)

The simulator should be capable of reporting:

    OpenSmell Simulator

    Odor: Coffee
    Representation: semantic

    Rendering capability:
      supported

    Rendering Coffee...

This milestone is not intended to demonstrate physical odor reproduction.

It demonstrates that the representation, parsing, rendering, mapping, and
device abstraction layers can operate independently.

---

## 15. Open Questions

The following questions remain intentionally unresolved:

- What should the `.osmell` serialization format contain?
- Should JSON be the canonical serialization format?
- How should representation models be uniquely identified?
- How should representation versions be handled?
- How should device capabilities be described?
- How should approximation quality be expressed?
- Should conversion between representation models be standardized?
- Which responsibilities belong to the Renderer versus the Mapper?
- How should MPEG-V interoperability be implemented?
- How should extensions be registered?
- How should unknown representation types be handled?
- What security considerations apply when controlling physical scent devices?

These questions will be addressed by subsequent RFCs.

---

## 16. Status of This Document

This document defines the initial vision of OpenSmell.

It is intentionally a Draft.

The architecture and terminology described here may change as prior art is
studied, prototypes are implemented, and feedback is received.

RFC-0001 should describe the long-term direction of OpenSmell rather than
freeze implementation details prematurely.
